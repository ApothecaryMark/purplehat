  <p class="lede">One billion units of the wealth disabled people are so often imagined to receive.</p>

  <p>While conducting research into financial trauma and disability, I came across a familiar stream of commentary portraying disabled people as scroungers: always looking for a handout and somehow getting rich on benefits.</p>

  <p>As someone with lived experience of physical disability, neurodiversity, mental ill-health and financial trauma, I decided to find out where all my alleged money was. Having looked down the back of the sofa and found nothing, I went down a full-blown ADHD rabbit hole and made a meme coin.</p>

  <div class="reveal" aria-label="The central idea of Disability Rich">
    <p>It is there. That is where all the millions are.</p>
  </div>

  <h2>The metaphor</h2>

  <p><em>Disability Rich</em> is a satirical conceptual artwork. Its apparent abundance is the point: being “rich” in tokens is not the same as having money, security or power.</p>

  <p>A billion units can be created, displayed and counted while saying almost nothing about whether the person holding them can pay the rent, build savings, obtain support or live independently. In that sense, the coin reflects a wider sleight of hand in public discussion: nominal entitlement is confused with material security, while the disabling structures that produce financial insecurity disappear from view.</p>

  <p>The work is grounded in the social model of disability. Disabled people are not made financially insecure by individual defect. Poverty and financial trauma are produced and intensified by disabling barriers, exclusion from work and public life, inadequate support, additional living costs, charging systems and rules that can punish saving.</p>

  <p>I intend to retain ten million units as an artist’s reserve. A further ten million units are intended for ten future allocations of one million each, potentially involving disabled people, disabled people’s organisations, charities, museums or cultural institutions as conversations around the work develop. Any allocation involving an individual will require informed consent and consideration of possible tax or means-tested-benefit consequences.</p>

  <p>I make no call to arms, ask nothing of the market and promise no return. I place the work into view as a metaphor for the disability financial experience. We will see what, if anything, happens to it.</p>
